# Accessible Audio

Audio content must be accessible to all users, including those who cannot hear the audio, those who use assistive technologies, and those who need keyboard-operable controls. This requires transcripts, accessible player interfaces, and careful handling of auto-play behavior.

## Overview

Accessible audio ensures that spoken content, music, and sound effects are available through text alternatives and that audio players are operable by all users. Unlike video, audio-only content requires transcripts rather than captions, since there is no visual component to synchronize with.

### Key Concepts

- **Transcripts**: Full text versions of all spoken content and relevant sound information
- **Accessible Player Controls**: Keyboard-operable playback, volume, and progress controls
- **Auto-play Prevention**: Audio should never start playing without user consent
- **Audio Alternatives**: Providing alternatives for audio content that conveys information

## WCAG Requirements

### Perceivable
- **1.1.1 Non-text Content**: Audio content must have a text alternative that serves the equivalent purpose
- **1.2.1 Audio-only and Video-only (Prerecorded)**: Prerecorded audio-only content must have a text transcript
- **1.2.8 Media Alternative (Prerecorded)**: A text transcript must be provided for all prerecorded media (Level AAA)

### Operable
- **2.1.1 Keyboard**: All audio player controls must be keyboard accessible
- **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide**: Auto-playing audio must have a mechanism to pause, stop, or hide it
- **1.4.2 Audio Control**: Auto-playing audio that lasts more than 3 seconds must have a pause, stop, or volume control mechanism

## Implementation Patterns

### Basic Audio Element with Controls

The native `<audio>` element with the `controls` attribute provides built-in, keyboard-accessible playback controls.

```html
<figure>
  <audio 
    id="podcast-episode" 
    controls
    aria-describedby="audio-description"
  >
    <source src="episode-42.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
    <source src="episode-42.ogg" type="audio/ogg">
    Your browser does not support the audio element.
  </audio>
  <figcaption id="audio-description">
    Episode 42 of the Accessibility Matters podcast: 
    "Understanding Screen Reader Navigation" — 12 minutes.
  </figcaption>
</figure>
```

### Transcript Markup

Provide a complete transcript for all audio content. Use a `<details>` element to keep the page tidy while making the transcript easily accessible.

```html
<div class="audio-component" role="region" aria-label="Audio player with transcript">
  <audio id="interview" controls>
    <source src="interview.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
  </audio>

  <details>
    <summary>
      <strong>Transcript: Interview with Accessibility Expert Dr. Chen</strong>
    </summary>
    <div class="transcript" role="region" aria-label="Audio transcript">
      <p>
        <strong>[0:00]</strong> 
        <em>[Theme music plays briefly and fades out]</em>
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Host:</strong> Welcome to today's interview. 
        I'm joined by Dr. Sarah Chen, a leading researcher in 
        web accessibility. Dr. Chen, thank you for being here.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>[0:08]</strong> 
        <strong>Dr. Chen:</strong> Thank you for having me. 
        I'm excited to discuss how we can make the web more inclusive.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>[0:14]</strong> 
        <strong>Host:</strong> Let's start with the basics. 
        What does accessibility mean in the context of web development?
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>[0:20]</strong> 
        <strong>Dr. Chen:</strong> Web accessibility means that 
        websites, tools, and technologies are designed and developed 
        so that people with disabilities can use them. 
        <em>[Pause]</em> More specifically, it means that people 
        can perceive, understand, navigate, and interact with the web.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>[0:35]</strong> 
        <em>[Brief musical interlude]</em>
      </p>
    </div>
  </details>
</div>
```

### Accessible Custom Audio Player

When building a custom audio player, ensure all controls are keyboard accessible and properly labeled.

```html
<div class="audio-player" role="region" aria-label="Audio player">
  <audio id="audio-source" preload="metadata">
    <source src="presentation.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
  </audio>

  <div class="player-controls" role="toolbar" aria-label="Audio controls">
    <button 
      type="button" 
      id="play-pause" 
      aria-label="Play audio"
      aria-pressed="false"
    >
      <svg aria-hidden="true" width="20" height="20" viewBox="0 0 20 20">
        <polygon points="5,3 17,10 5,17" fill="currentColor"/>
      </svg>
    </button>

    <button 
      type="button" 
      id="stop-btn" 
      aria-label="Stop audio"
    >
      <svg aria-hidden="true" width="20" height="20" viewBox="0 0 20 20">
        <rect x="3" y="3" width="14" height="14" fill="currentColor"/>
      </svg>
    </button>

    <button 
      type="button" 
      id="rewind-btn" 
      aria-label="Rewind 10 seconds"
    >
      <svg aria-hidden="true" width="20" height="20" viewBox="0 0 20 20">
        <path d="M10 4 L2 10 L10 16 M18 4 L10 10 L18 16" fill="currentColor"/>
      </svg>
    </button>

    <button 
      type="button" 
      id="forward-btn" 
      aria-label="Forward 10 seconds"
    >
      <svg aria-hidden="true" width="20" height="20" viewBox="0 0 20 20">
        <path d="M2 4 L10 10 L2 16 M10 4 L18 10 L10 16" fill="currentColor"/>
      </svg>
    </button>

    <div class="volume-control" role="group" aria-label="Volume control">
      <button 
        type="button" 
        id="mute-btn" 
        aria-label="Mute audio"
        aria-pressed="false"
      >
        <svg aria-hidden="true" width="20" height="20" viewBox="0 0 20 20">
          <path d="M3 7h4l5-4v14l-5-4H3z" fill="currentColor"/>
        </svg>
      </button>
      <label for="volume-slider" class="sr-only">Volume</label>
      <input 
        type="range" 
        id="volume-slider" 
        min="0" 
        max="1" 
        step="0.1" 
        value="1"
      >
    </div>

    <div class="progress-control" role="group" aria-label="Audio progress">
      <label for="progress-slider" class="sr-only">Progress</label>
      <input 
        type="range" 
        id="progress-slider" 
        min="0" 
        max="100" 
        step="1" 
        value="0"
        aria-label="Audio progress percentage"
      >
    </div>

    <span id="time-display" aria-live="off" aria-label="Current time">
      0:00 / 12:30
    </span>
  </div>
</div>
```

### Audio with Multiple Sources

Provide audio in multiple formats for browser compatibility, and clearly label each source.

```html
<audio controls aria-label="Pronunciation guide: 'Accessibility'">
  <source src="accessibility.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
  <source src="accessibility.ogg" type="audio/ogg">
  <source src="accessibility.wav" type="audio/wav">
  <p>
    Your browser does not support the audio element. 
    <a href="accessibility.mp3">Download the audio file</a>.
  </p>
</audio>
```

### Preventing Auto-play

Audio should never start automatically. Always require user interaction to begin playback.

```html
<!-- Bad: Auto-playing audio -->
<audio src="background-music.mp3" autoplay loop></audio>

<!-- Good: User-initiated playback -->
<audio id="podcast" controls preload="none">
  <source src="podcast-episode.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
</audio>

<!-- If audio must auto-play (e.g., notification), provide immediate stop -->
<div class="notification-audio" role="alert">
  <audio id="notification-sound" src="alert.mp3" autoplay></audio>
  <button 
    type="button" 
    onclick="document.getElementById('notification-sound').pause()"
    aria-label="Stop notification sound"
  >
    Stop sound
  </button>
</div>
```

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. Auto-playing Audio Without Controls

```html
<!-- Bad: Auto-playing with no way to stop -->
<audio src="music.mp3" autoplay loop></audio>

<!-- Good: Provide controls and no auto-play -->
<audio src="music.mp3" controls preload="metadata">
  <source src="music.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
</audio>
```

### 2. Missing Transcripts

```html
<!-- Bad: Audio with no text alternative -->
<audio src="lecture.mp3" controls></audio>

<!-- Good: Audio with transcript link or inline transcript -->
<audio src="lecture.mp3" controls></audio>
<details>
  <summary>View transcript</summary>
  <div class="transcript">
    <p>Full transcript content here...</p>
  </div>
</details>
```

### 3. No Pause or Stop Control

```html
<!-- Bad: Short audio clip with no controls -->
<audio src="notification.mp3" autoplay></audio>

<!-- Good: Provide a way to stop the audio -->
<button 
  type="button" 
  id="stop-notification"
  aria-label="Stop notification sound"
>
  Stop
</button>
```

### 4. Using Divs or Spans as Player Controls

```html
<!-- Bad: Non-semantic controls -->
<div class="play" onclick="playAudio()">▶</div>
<span class="stop" onclick="stopAudio()">⏹</span>

<!-- Good: Button elements with accessible labels -->
<button type="button" aria-label="Play audio" onclick="playAudio()">
  <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- play icon --></svg>
</button>
<button type="button" aria-label="Stop audio" onclick="stopAudio()">
  <svg aria-hidden="true"><!-- stop icon --></svg>
</button>
```

### 5. Missing Audio Description in Context

Audio content embedded in a page should have surrounding context that explains what the audio is about, even before the user interacts with it.

```html
<!-- Bad: Audio with no context -->
<audio src="interview.mp3" controls></audio>

<!-- Good: Audio with descriptive context -->
<div class="audio-section">
  <h3>Interview: The Future of Web Standards</h3>
  <p>A 15-minute conversation with W3C representative about upcoming changes to web standards.</p>
  <audio src="interview.mp3" controls>
    <source src="interview.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
  </audio>
  <details>
    <summary>Read transcript</summary>
    <!-- Transcript content -->
  </details>
</div>
```

## Testing Checklist

### Transcript Availability
- [ ] A transcript is available for all prerecorded audio content
- [ ] The transcript includes all spoken words
- [ ] Speaker identification is included when multiple speakers are present
- [ ] Relevant sound effects and music are noted in the transcript
- [ ] Timestamps are provided for longer audio content
- [ ] The transcript is easily discoverable (linked or inline)

### Keyboard Control
- [ ] Play/pause button is reachable via Tab
- [ ] Stop button is reachable via Tab
- [ ] Volume control is operable with arrow keys
- [ ] Progress bar is operable with arrow keys
- [ ] All controls have visible focus indicators
- [ ] Space bar toggles play/pause when the player is focused

### Screen Reader Compatibility
- [ ] The audio player region is announced
- [ ] Play/pause button announces its current state
- [ ] Volume control announces its current value
- [ ] Progress indicator announces current position
- [ ] The transcript is accessible via screen reader navigation
- [ ] Audio does not auto-play and interfere with screen reader output
